Prop-N-Lock

OBJECTIVE:

Allow wheelchair users who reside at the Sertoma Centre to independently dispose of their household waste in a large communal dumpster. Currently, this task is difficiult due to the need to simultaneoulsy physically elevate themselves on the wheelchair, open the large lid, and also lift the trash container. An image of the Sertoma Centre dumpster area is shown below:

Phase 1: Research

Conducted secondary, web-based research and seperate primary research interviews with Mr. Dave Stubitsch and with Jen. Through these, we developed three main design constraints. The design must:

  • Be able to be used independently by wheelchair users
  • Be sanitary (minimal contact with dumpster)
  • Not permanently attach to dumpster (so that the dumpster can be emptied safely)
  • Be durable throughout summer and winter weather


Phase 2: Brainstorming and Mockups

Held a team brainstorming session and chose 5 ideas to pursue in the form of initial mockups:

The one with best performance was a rope-and-pulley system (middle-bottom). Sketches of the concept below:

The team presented the rope-and-pulley system during a design review and recieved feedback on our design. Based on this feedback, we enhanced our design by making the design more weatherproof (use an insulated cable, enclose the winch, etc.). Changes are shown below:


PHASE 3: FINAL REPORT, PROTOTYPE, AND PRESENTATION

Our final deliverable was a prototype of the design (the components of which could be dissassembled and used directly for the final design if desired). We purchased all materials online, and then assembled them together, using plywoood as a mockup of the enclosure and dumpster lid. Finally, my team and I synthesized our design choices and findings into a Final Report. Then, we presented our design and deliverables to our client.
